This image displays a horizontal narrative handscroll in ink and rich colour on silk, depicting an aristocratic night banquet unfolding in successive scenes, presented in stacked horizontal sections. It is the most celebrated surviving example of Chinese domestic genre and portrait painting of the Five Dynasties period.

1. The First Scene (Listening to the Lute)
A gaunt, long-bearded official in dark robes and a tall black hat sits on a couch at the head of a large bed-couch, watching a performance. Facing him, a lady in red plays the pipa (lute) while seated guests lean forward, absorbed. Servants and ladies-in-waiting cluster behind screens.

2. The Second Scene (Watching the Dancers)
The host changes into plain monk-like robes and beats a drum for a dancer performing the "six reversed limbs" dance; a monk guest looks away in embarrassment while other guests watch intently.

3. The Third Scene (Resting)
The host washes his hands between scenes; maids bring water and towels; a small pavilion interior with a candle-lit bed glows behind.

4. The Fourth Scene (Listening to the Flute)
The host, now seated on a low chair informally dressed, listens to five female musicians playing flutes and pipes arranged on a carpet; a servant girl talks into his ear.

5. The Fifth Scene (The Farewell)
The host stands with palm raised in farewell as guests depart with lanterns; a night sky implied by the quiet, dim palette.

Style and Details
Interiors are articulated with screens, couches, tables, tall candlestands and hanging drums; costume colours — vermilion, azure, pale green, white — are laid flat with fine iron-wire outlines. Faces are individualised portraits: the host's heavy, weary features recur in every scene. Red-lacquered furniture, blue-green screens and scholarly objects furnish the rooms.

Overall Impression
The scroll is simultaneously a record of sumptuous entertainment and a covert psychological portrait: commissioned by a ruler to probe whether his minister was feigning dissipation, it immortalises one man's melancholy amid the revelry. It is prized as a first-rank monument of figure painting and material culture.
