This image displays a segmented, horizontal narrative handscroll on aged, beige-toned silk, presented in stacked horizontal sections. It illustrates a famous lyrical prose-poem (fu) about a goddess and a mortal prince, in the gentle colour-wash idiom of early Chinese figure painting.

1. The Opening (The Meeting)
The scroll opens with a retinue travelling through wooded hills: carriages, riders and banner-bearers advance along a riverbank. In the water and mist beyond, a graceful female deity begins to appear — the Goddess of the River Luo.

2. The Encounter (Center)
The prince and the goddess face one another across the water amid drifting clouds:
- The goddess is shown as a slender, floating figure with trailing gauze robes and a long sash caught by the wind, moving weightlessly above the waves; her hair is gathered high and her gaze is downcast and melancholy.
- Attendant deities and companions disport themselves nearby: some gather lotus, some fish with nets, some ride small skiffs among lotus leaves; cranes, dragons and fish-animate monsters of the river swirl in the currents.
- A bright red sun disc hangs in the upper sky of one section, balanced by a crescent moon elsewhere.

3. The Parting (Later Sections)
The goddess departs in a chariot drawn by dragons amid clouds while the prince's retinue stands on the far bank; a shaman or attendant holds a tall staff. The final sections show the travellers returning through the hills past fantastic rocks and twin trees, the procession growing smaller and quieter until it exits the scroll.

4. Style
Trees are rendered in archaic outline patterns; mountains are decorative rather than monumental; figures are evenly spaced in slow rhythmic succession. Colour is restrained — pale greens, ochres and iron-red accents over fine ink lines.

Overall Impression
The scroll translates a tragic chance-meeting between mortal and goddess into a sequence of elegant, dreamlike tableaux. It survives only in later copies of a lost 4th-century original and remains the most famous early narrative illustration in Chinese painting.
