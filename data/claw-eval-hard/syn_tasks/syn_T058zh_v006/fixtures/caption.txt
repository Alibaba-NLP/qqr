This image displays a long horizontal handscroll in ink and colour on silk, depicting a great herd of horses at pasture in a landscape, presented in stacked horizontal sections. It is the most famous work of equine painting in Chinese art, executed by a court painter from Milan who served three Qing emperors.

1. The Composition
Across the scroll, roughly a hundred horses — bay, chestnut, white, piebald, dapple-grey and black — are distributed among groves of ancient pines and autumn-lit deciduous trees, rolling banks and a broad shallow pool in the foreground.

2. Key Details
- Horses: Each animal is individualised in pose and colour: some stand resting, some graze, some roll in the grass, others splash into the water to bathe; mares with foals shelter beneath the trees; a pair rears playfully; distant groups diminish into atmospheric haze along the horizon.
- Herders: Grooms and herdsmen in Manchu-style riding dress watch from the banks; one in a brown jacket leads a mount by the halter; another squats at the water's edge.
- Landscape: Huge, gnarled trees with twisting trunks dominate the middle distance — pines in deep green, maples and oaks in rusty red and gold; a weeping willow trails over the left bank; low hills fade into a warm, luminous distance.
- Light and Air: The warm yellow of the sky and the long shadows suggest late afternoon; Western-style modelling gives the horses volume and the trees cast soft shadows.

3. Style
The painting fuses Chinese silk-scroll format and tree idioms with European chiaroscuro, anatomical precision and single-point atmospheric perspective — the hallmark of the Italian Jesuit painter at the Chinese court. A small collector seal appears on the mounting; a modern publisher's watermark is visible at the lower right corner of this reproduction.

Overall Impression
The scroll reads as an imperial statement of abundance: a hundred splendid steeds — symbols of the dynasty's cavalry and vigour — at ease in an idealised autumn parkland. It is universally admired as the pinnacle of Sino-European court painting.
