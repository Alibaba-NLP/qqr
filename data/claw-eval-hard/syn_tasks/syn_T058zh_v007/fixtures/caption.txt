This image displays a short horizontal handscroll in ink and mineral colour on aged, dark-toned silk, presented with an inscription panel and the painting itself. It is the earliest extant landscape scroll on silk and the founding monument of the blue-and-green (qinglü) landscape tradition, painted in the brief dynasty that reunified China before the Tang.

1. The Title Panel (Right)
At the right end stands a vertical panel of large formal characters giving the work's title, written in an archaic hand, followed by the painter's name; beside and below it are stacked red collector seals, including the great square reviewing seal of the Qianlong emperor and an imperial library seal. The title characters read (top to bottom): the painter's name followed by three characters meaning "Spring Outing Scroll".

2. The Landscape
- Mountains rise in rounded then sharp peaks at both ends, rendered with vigorous ink outlines filled with azurite blue and malachite green; cliffs are faceted with oblique texture strokes and a faint wash of ochre along the sunlit faces.
- A long river runs across the middle ground, its banks lined with budding willows and blossoming trees; slender boats with punters cross the water.
- Tiny figures — gentlemen on horseback and foot, a lady in a small open pavilion watching the water, travellers on the foreshore — animate the springtime excursion; a causeway with a small wooden bridge leads into the hills.
- Clouds of pale blue wash drift against the slopes; the whole surface has darkened with age to a warm brown, against which the blues and greens still glow.

3. Style
Space is stacked in the archaic "upper-ridge, lower-view" manner without overlapping mist planes; trees are symmetrical coral-like forms; figures are minute and calligraphic. The interest is in colour, rhythm and noble decoration rather than realistic depth.

Overall Impression
The scroll captures the leisurely joy of a spring outing among blue-green hills and new willows. As the sole surviving attributed work of the founder of landscape painting in China, it has been treasured by successive imperial collections for over a millennium.
